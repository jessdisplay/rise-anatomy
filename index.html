2Quality indicators

Conflict of interest policies are provided or explained to each participant using the language, mode of communication and terms that the participant is most likely to understand.

Each participant is supported to understand the distinction between the provision of specialised support coordination and other reasonable and necessary supports funded under a participant’s plan using the language, mode of communication and terms that the participant is most likely to understand.

Referrals to and from other providers are documented for each participant.

Specialised Support Coordination Policy

Policy. What we commit to, and why.

1Purpose

This policy sets out what [Provider name] commits to when we provide . It is for whose situation carries high risk or complexity, their families and advocates, our , and the people who check our work.

2Applies to

  • Every participant who receives specialised support coordination from [Provider name], and every person who asks us for it, before they sign (the Registration Rules, Schedule 6, clause 1(2)(a)).
  • Everyone who works for us on that support: employees, contractors, agency workers, volunteers and key personnel.
  • The whole of the coordination: assessing , planning for a , finding and briefing , monitoring, and reporting.
  • Any other support we or anyone connected with us provide to the same participant. Commitment 13 keeps it separate from the coordination.

Short names used here:

  • "The Registration Rules" means the National Disability Insurance Scheme (Provider Registration and Practice Standards) Rules 2018.
  • "The Incident Rules" means the National Disability Insurance Scheme (Incident Management and Reportable Incidents) Rules 2018.
  • "The Code" means the National Disability Insurance Scheme (Code of Conduct) Rules 2018, section 6.

3Commitments

C1

We will allocate each participant a coordinator who is qualified, trained and signed off as competent for that participant's risks and complexity. A coordinator holds [qualification and years of experience we require]. The expects specialised support coordination to be delivered by a qualified and experienced practitioner, such as a social worker, psychologist, occupational therapist or mental health nurse, or a person with equivalent demonstrated experience in complex situations; set the requirement at adoption with that in mind. They have completed our training on the risk factors listed in FRM-SCO-01 . They have supervision with the Service Manager at least every month.

C2

We will assess each participant's risks and complexity with them. We use their own account and the risk factors and rating scale in FRM-SCO-01 Risk and Crisis Response Plan. At the first meeting we agree interim safety steps and set a provisional risk level. We make the plan within the time for that level in PRO-SCO-02 Decision table 2, counted from the first meeting: 5 for extreme risk, 10 for high, 15 for medium and low. A second person, the , reads and challenges the assessment and plan: before the plan is issued for a participant at high or extreme risk, and within 10 business days of issue for every other participant.

C3

We will make a Risk and Crisis Response Plan with each participant, in their words and in a form they can use. It names their warning signs, what helps, and who does what if a crisis or a happens. We give the participant their copy. We review the plan at the interval for their risk level, and after any crisis, incident or major change.

C4

We will always have someone who has read the participant's plan and can respond to a crisis. That is the coordinator, or the when the coordinator is away. In our hours a coordinator or cover who has read the plan answers a crisis call and starts the plan within 2 hours. Outside our hours the participant's plan names who they call, and we say truthfully whether that is us.

C5

We will consult the participant when we plan and coordinate supports, and before each plan review. With their consent, we also consult their and . We record who the participant agreed we may talk to and what we may tell them. We respect a refusal.

C6

We will identify suitable providers for each support. We check registration where it applies, evidence of skills and experience for the participant's kind of situation, and fit with their preferences. We follow one options rule, set in POL-SCO-02 C4: at least three suitable options able to meet the need, of which at least two have no relationship to us, where that many exist, and we say which have a relationship to us. Where fewer exist we record who we asked and tell the participant why.

C7

We will make sure every provider who carries out the plan understands the participant's risks and their own part before their first support. We brief each provider on the parts of the plan the participant agreed to share. We get their confirmation in writing.

C8

We will bring the participant's providers and other services together to work as one team. We hold a coordination meeting at the interval for the participant's risk level. We hold another within 5 business days after any crisis, incident or breakdown of supports. Every action has an owner and a date.

C9

We will monitor each participant's plan. We contact the participant at the interval for their risk level and check that each support is delivered as planned. When a support is not delivered as planned we act within 5 business days.

C10

We will check each participant's plan budget and plan dates every month, in the first 5 business days of the month. For each funding category we compare the share used and booked with the share of plan time elapsed. A gap of more than 15 percentage points is explained, and the participant is told within 5 business days, and their plan manager too if they have one. This is the same monthly funds check as POL-SCO-02 C11. It is done once, under PRO-SCO-02 step 22, and recorded on FRM-SCO-02 Section H. When a plan is within 12 weeks of its reassessment date and no review has been scheduled, we ask the NDIA to confirm one.

C11

We will meet every reporting obligation attached to the participant's plan. We list each report and notice owed when coordination starts: the NDIA's implementation report, due 8 weeks after coordination starts; mid-term reports at the frequency the request for service sets; and the plan reassessment report before the plan review, which we draft with the participant 8 weeks before the reassessment date and send 6 weeks before it, or by the date the NDIA sets. Due dates come from the request for service and the provider portal. We show the participant each report about them before we send it. We send it by its due date, or tell the recipient before the date passes if we will miss it.

C12

We will arrange supports as the participant directs, using their plan funds for the purposes they intend. We give them their options in the language and form they understand, and build their skills to manage their own supports if they wish. POL-SCO-02 Management of a Participant's NDIS Supports Policy sets this out.

C13

We will keep specialised support coordination separate from any other support we, or anyone connected with us, provide. We give each participant our conflict of policy and its plain summary in the format they use, and check they can say back what it means. We explain, and check they can say back, how coordination differs from the other supports in their plan. We take no payment, gift or benefit for naming or referring a provider. We tell the participant about any interest we hold before they choose, and their choice of provider never changes their coordination. We record every referral to or from another provider in REG-SCO-02 Support Coordination Referral Register. PRO-SCO-01 Support Coordination Conflict of Interest Procedure sets how.

C14

We will not leave a participant without a plan when coordination ends or changes. Before our last day we hand the Risk and Crisis Response Plan to the next coordinator, with the participant's consent. We also give the participant their copy.

4Ground rules

G1

The participant leads. Their words, their choices and their pace come first. We never decide for a participant who can decide for themselves.

G2

We share a participant's information only with the people they have agreed to. The exceptions are a call to 000, a legal duty, and a serious threat to life, health or safety when we cannot ask first. We tell the participant at the start that these exceptions exist, and tell them if we use one.

G3

A coordinator is not a clinician or a behaviour support practitioner. We pass health and behaviour questions to the participant's practitioner. We never write a restrictive practice into a plan. Only an authorised behaviour support plan can hold one. PRO-BSP-02 Regulated Restrictive Practices Procedure applies if someone asks.

G4

A participant who says no has made a decision. We record it, ask again at the next review, and never end coordination because of it.

G5

We say our hours truthfully. We do not suggest we are a crisis service unless we staff one. The participant's plan says who they call outside our hours.

G6

Nobody at [Provider name] can extend a time limit that the law sets.

G7

A participant at high or extreme risk never goes without interim safety steps, a cover coordinator and a named person to call.

G8

A concern about a coordinator goes to the Service Manager. A concern about the Service Manager goes to the Chief Executive. A concern about the Chief Executive goes to the chair of the governing body. In a provider with one owner it goes to the . Anyone can also phone the NDIS Commission on 1800 035 544.

G9

In a small provider one person may hold several roles. A sole trader holds every role except the case reviewer, who is a person outside the business. The cover coordinator may be a coordinator from another provider, engaged in writing. The independent contact for concerns may be the case reviewer. The case reviewer also gives the sole trader supervision at least every month, reads the register as REG-SCO-01 sets out, and does the checks in "How we check" that would otherwise be the sole trader checking their own work. The sole trader engages the case reviewer and any outside cover in writing, with a confidentiality undertaking and agreed response times, names them on adoption and tells each participant who they are.

G10

The system does not depend on any one software product. Paper works when systems are down.

G11

Every participant is told how to complain, and how to change their coordinator or a provider. We tell them at the first meeting and at each plan review. POL-FBK-01 Feedback and Complaints Management Policy sets how we handle a complaint.

9Words we use

specialised support coordination
help for a participant whose situation carries high risk or complexity. It means putting their NDIS plan into action, monitoring it and reviewing it, and reducing the risk and complexity of their situation.
participant
a person with disability who receives specialised support coordination from us, or who is asking us for it.
NDIS plan
the plan the NDIA approves for the participant, with its funding and dates.
NDIA
the National Disability Insurance Agency, which runs the NDIS and approves NDIS plans. The Quality Indicators call it "the Agency".
coordinator
the worker who does the coordination with a participant.
cover coordinator
a second coordinator, with the same sign-off, who acts when the coordinator is away. For a sole trader this may be a coordinator from another provider, engaged in writing.
case reviewer
a person who reads the assessment and plan for each participant, and challenges them. They are not the coordinator for that participant. For a sole trader the case reviewer is a person outside the business.
Risk and Crisis Response Plan
the plan we make with the participant in FRM-SCO-01. It holds their risks, warning signs, what helps and the steps to take in a crisis.
risk and complexity
the things in a participant's life that could seriously harm them or others. It also means anything that makes their supports hard to arrange or keep steady.
crisis
a point where someone is at risk of serious harm, or where supports the participant depends on have broken down or are about to.
breakdown of supports
a support the participant relies on stops, or is not delivered as planned, and the participant is at risk or worse off.
critical support
a support that would put the participant at risk if it stopped, named in FRM-SCO-01 Risk and Crisis Response Plan.
provider
an NDIS provider, registered or not, who gives a support in the participant's NDIS plan.
mainstream service
a service for the whole community, such as health, housing, justice, child safety, mental health, education or transport.
support network
the people in the participant's life whom they choose to involve, such as family, friends, carers and advocates.
independent advocate
a person outside [Provider name] who speaks up for the participant's wishes and rights.
decision maker
a person with legal authority to make decisions for a participant, such as a guardian, administrator or nominee.
interest
ownership of a provider, a shared owner or director, a family tie, a referral arrangement, or any payment, gift or benefit from a provider.
independent contact for concerns
in a provider with one owner, the outside person who receives concerns about the owner. REG-GOV-01 Delegations Register names them. This may be the case reviewer.
business day
a day other than a Saturday, a Sunday or a public holiday in the state or territory where the participant lives.

Support Coordination Conflict of Interest Procedure

Procedure. Who does what, in what order, and when.

1Purpose

The result: every who uses [Provider name] for support coordination hears our conflict of policy. They hear about every interest we hold in a support they may choose. They hear that their choice of never changes their coordination. Every referral to or from another provider is on the record.

This procedure gives effect to POL-SCO-01 Policy, C13, and to the options rule in POL-SCO-02 Management of a Participant's NDIS Supports Policy, C4. It is also the support coordination part of the provider's Conflict of Interest Policy (POL-GOV-02).

An interest is anything that [Provider name], a worker, or a person close to either could gain from a participant's choice of provider. It can be money, a business, a home, a favour or a referral arrangement. A referral is each time we pass a participant to another provider, or another provider or person passes a participant to us.

In this procedure:

  • "coordination" means specialised support coordination
  • "the Registration Rules" means the National Disability Insurance Scheme (Provider Registration and Practice Standards) Rules 2018
  • "the Code of Conduct Rules" means the National Disability Insurance Scheme (Code of Conduct) Rules 2018.
  • the : a day other than a Saturday, a Sunday or a public holiday in the state or territory where the participant lives.

2When and who

Start this procedure when any of these happens:

  • A person asks us for support coordination (steps 1 to 13).
  • A participant needs a support from another provider, or asks to change provider (steps 14 to 18, 20 to 22 and 24 to 27).
  • Someone refers a participant to us (steps 14, 19 to 21 and 23 to 27).
  • A plan review comes up, 12 months pass, or one of our interests changes (step 28).

A person who has not yet signed with us is covered in the same way as a participant (the Registration Rules, Schedule 6, clause 1(2)(a)).

Two procedures send you here. PRO-SCO-02 Specialised Support Coordination Procedure step 14 does it before a provider is named. PRO-SCO-03 Management of a Participant's NDIS Supports Procedure step 12 does it when we hold an interest in an option. Use FRM-SCO-02 Support Options and Choice Record for the detail of each option. This procedure adds our interest, the disclosure and the referral record.

Who acts:

  • The does steps 1 to 11, 14 to 26 and 28.
  • The Service Manager owns this procedure and does steps 12, 13, 27 and 29.
  • The Chief Executive keeps REG-GOV-02 Conflict of Interest Register and decides each interest of [Provider name] under PRO-GOV-01 Delegations and Decision-Making Procedure.
  • The Quality Manager does step 30.

A sole trader is the coordinator, the Service Manager and the Chief Executive. The , a person outside the business named on adoption (POL-SCO-01 ground rule 9), reads the rows every 3 months at step 29 and decides any interest the sole trader holds. Name an in REG-GOV-01 Delegations Register to receive concerns about you; this may be the case reviewer. A provider that offers coordination only still follows every step. Its list of interests then says "none held".

3Before you start

  • You can open REG-GOV-02 and REG-SCO-02 Support Coordination Referral Register.
  • You have FRM-SCO-03 Support Coordination Conflict of Interest Disclosure Record.
  • You have STA-GOV-01 Conflict of Interest: Plain Summary and STA-SCO-01 Specialised Support Coordination: Plain Summary, in the formats your participants use, and the full POL-GOV-02, where the provider has adopted them. Until then, you explain the policy and the difference in plain words (steps 4 and 5 give the words) and record "explained, no document given".
  • You know where the participant's communication needs are kept: FRM-RGT-01 Communication, Advocacy and Support Preferences Form and REG-RGT-01 Communication and Accessible Information Register.
  • You know who to call for an : [local advocacy service and phone].
  • You know how to reach the Service Manager today: [Service Manager phone].

Separation of functions: where we also provide other supports, a coordinator is not supervised by, set targets by, or paid by reference to the other service; coordination records are held apart from the other service's records; and a participant may ask for a coordinator from outside the organisation, and we help them find one. The Chief Executive records the arrangement in REG-GOV-02, and the coordinator tells the participant about it at step 6 (FRM-SCO-03 Q12).

4Watch out

  • Telling a participant late takes away their choice. A participant who hears about our interest after they choose did not choose freely.
  • A steer is a conflict even when nobody means it. Putting our service first, praising it, or leaving out other options is steering. It can breach the Code of Conduct Rules, section 6(1)(d), which requires integrity, honesty and transparency.
  • Coordination never changes because of a choice. Cutting hours, slowing replies or ending coordination after a participant picks another provider punishes them for choosing. They can complain to the NDIS Commission on 1800 035 544.
  • "Do you understand?" proves nothing. A yes can mean "I want this to end". Ask the participant to say it in their own words.
  • A signature is not understanding. A participant who signs without having heard each interest has not been told.
  • Nothing is paid for a referral. A payment, commission, referral fee, gift or favour for a referral, given or taken, creates a conflict. Our rule is stricter than the Code of Conduct Rules: we take none and we give none.

Law changing: from 20 November 2026, a new section 73VA of the National Disability Insurance Scheme Act 2013 makes it an offence and a civil penalty for an NDIS provider to give or offer any gift or benefit likely to induce a person to use its services. Our rule already bans payment for referrals, given or taken, and is wider. Check the section when it starts.

5Steps

Prepare

Step 1The coordinator

Find out how the participant communicates best, who decides for them and who they want with them.

8Done means

A disclosure is done when all of these are true:

  • the participant holds our conflict of interest summary in a format they use
  • the participant has said back, in their own words, what it means and how coordination differs from other supports
  • the participant has heard about each interest of ours, before choosing any provider
  • the participant has heard that their choice never changes their coordination
  • the decision, the acknowledgement and the date for the next disclosure are recorded
  • the Service Manager has checked the record.

A referral is done when all of these are true:

  • the participant chose from a real range of options, and heard about each interest of ours before choosing
  • nobody paid or was paid for the referral
  • information went only after the participant agreed
  • the participant knows the outcome
  • the follow-up shows the result, and the participant's coordination has not changed
  • the Service Manager has closed the row.

These records now exist:

  • a completed FRM-SCO-03, acknowledged as question 17 records, on the participant's file for each disclosure and each referral
  • a row in REG-SCO-02 for each of them
  • an entry in REG-GOV-02 (participants told of each interest) for each interest we told the participant about
  • a row in REG-RGT-01 for the document given
  • a consent record in FRM-INF-01 for each time information was shared.

Support Coordination Conflict of Interest Disclosure Record

Form. What we write down at the moment it happens.

1What this is for

Use this record to show that a heard three things, in a way they understand. First, how we handle conflicts of . Second, which interests we hold in the supports they may choose. Third, that their choice of never changes their coordination. It also records each referral to or from another provider, and what happened to it.

The completes it with the participant, at the time. Do not write it up later from memory.

  • Section A: every record.
  • Section B: a disclosure record. Complete it at the first meeting where supports are discussed, at each plan review, every 12 months, and when one of our interests changes.
  • Sections C and D: a referral record. Start a new copy for each referral, whether it is out or in. Complete Section C as you put the options and as the participant chooses. Complete Section D when the outcome is known and at the follow-up.
  • Section E: the Service Manager completes it for every record.

Have ready:

  • the participant's communication needs
  • the list of our interests from REG-GOV-02 Conflict of Interest Register
  • STA-GOV-01 Conflict of Interest: Plain Summary and STA-SCO-01 : Plain Summary, in the participant's format, where the provider has adopted them; until then, the plain words in PRO-SCO-01 steps 4 and 5
  • an interpreter or advocate, if the participant wants one.

Ask the participant themself. Ask a family member or other supporter only when the participant wants that, or when that person is their legal . Tell the participant everything either way.

What happens next: the Service Manager checks the record and enters it in REG-SCO-02 Support Coordination Referral Register and REG-GOV-02. PRO-SCO-01 Support Coordination Conflict of Interest Procedure sets every step.

2Privacy

Read this to the participant.

We collect this information for three reasons. It shows that you were told about our interests before you chose. It keeps a record of each referral made for you. It meets the NDIS Practice Standards and the NDIS Code of Conduct.

Your coordinator, the Service Manager, the Chief Executive and the Quality Manager read it. An auditor or the NDIS Commission may see it as the law allows. We share your information with another provider only when you agree, or when a law requires or allows it. FRM-INF-01 Consent to Collect, Use and Share Information Form records what you agree to.

You can ask to see what we hold and ask us to correct it. You can ask us to stop sharing. We keep this record for 7 years after it is closed, or longer where section 45B of the NDIS Act 2013 requires. POL-INF-01 Information Management Policy sets how we store and protect it.

1What this proves

This register is the running record of what each heard about our conflicts of , and of every referral to or from another . A disclosure row shows the policy we gave, the interests we told them about and the promise that their choice never changes their coordination. A referral row shows the options, our relationship to each one, what the participant chose, and what happened next.

It holds three kinds of row:

  • a disclosure: each time we go through our conflict of interest policy and our interests with a participant
  • a referral out: a participant is sent to, or helped to choose, another provider
  • a referral in: another provider or person refers a participant to us.

In this register:

  • "the Registration Rules" means the National Disability Insurance Scheme (Provider Registration and Practice Standards) Rules 2018
  • "the Code of Conduct Rules" means the National Disability Insurance Scheme (Code of Conduct) Rules 2018.
  • Indicators evidenced: s 66 (1), s 66 (2), s 66 (3) and s 66 (4). The register also supports s 6 (2), s 9 (4), s 9 (5), s 11 (8), s 14 (1) and s 22 (2).
  • Legal basis: the Registration Rules, Schedule 6, clause 5, which gives each participant transparent, factual advice about their support options that promotes choice and control. Clause 1(2)(a) applies the same duty to a person who has not yet become a participant. The Code of Conduct Rules, section 6(1)(a) and (d), require respect for decision-making, and integrity, honesty and transparency.
  • No NDIS rule sets the columns of this register, so the columns are our standard. Section 45B of the NDIS Act 2013 (from 27 August 2026) requires records relating to claimed supports to be kept for 7 years; our retention standard meets it.
  • Retention: keep each row for 7 years after it is closed (our standard). For a participant who was under 18 when the row was made, keep it until they turn 25 when that is later.

Which columns apply:

  • Every row fills columns A to J and AK to AM.
  • A disclosure row also fills columns K to T. Columns U to AJ say "not applicable".
  • A referral out row fills columns U to AJ. Columns K to T say "not applicable".
  • A referral in row fills columns U, Y to AA and AC to AJ. Columns V to X and AB say "not applicable".
  • Column AN is filled when a row is closed.

A blank is never an answer. Write "none", "no" or "not applicable".

3Entry rules

  1. The coordinator adds the row. When the coordinator is away, the Service Manager names another coordinator the same day.
  2. Add a disclosure row within 2 of the meeting (our standard). Add a referral row on the day the referral starts or arrives (our standard).
  3. Use one row for each disclosure meeting and one row for each referral. A participant who needs three supports from three providers has three referral rows.
  4. Write the participant number, never the name. The name stays on FRM-SCO-03 and on the participant's file.
  5. Fill column AB before the participant chooses, not after. The date in column AB must be on or before the date in column AC. When it is later, the Service Manager writes "late disclosure" in column AL.
  6. Record an offer of a payment, commission, referral fee, gift or favour in column AD even when it was refused. The offer is the event.
  7. Never overwrite or delete an entry. A correction is a new version of the row, with the date, your name and the reason.
  8. When the coordinator holds an interest, the coordinator who took over enters the row. Column J shows the FRM-GOV-01 record number.
  9. Reopen a closed row when the participant changes their choice or raises a concern about it. Set column AM to "open" and record the reason in column AK.
  10. A participant who makes a new choice is a new referral row. Link it to the earlier row in column AK.

A disclosure row stays "current" until the next disclosure row replaces it. Then the Service Manager sets it to "closed".

A referral row is closed only when all of these are true:

  • every required column is filled, or says "not applicable"
  • the participant was told about each interest of ours before they chose, and column AB shows it
  • nobody paid or was paid for the referral, or column AD shows the offer was refused and declared
  • information went only after the consent in column AE
  • the participant was told the outcome, and column AH shows when and how
  • the follow-up in column AI is done and shows the result
  • column AJ shows the participant's coordination did not change, or shows what changed and the action taken
  • the Service Manager has checked the row and signed column AL.
