Specialised Support Coordination Policy

  1. 1

    Purpose

    Why this exists and for whom, in one or two sentences.

    This policy sets out what [Provider name] commits to when we provide specialised support coordination. It is for participants whose situation carries high risk or complexity, their families and advocates, our coordinators, and the people who check our work.

  2. 2

    Applies to

    Who it covers, and which supports or settings.

    • Every participant who receives specialised support coordination from [Provider name], and every person who asks us for it, before they sign (the Registration Rules, Schedule 6, clause 1(2)(a)).
    • Everyone who works for us on that support: employees, contractors, agency workers, volunteers and key personnel.
    • The whole of the coordination: assessing risk and complexity, planning for a crisis, finding and briefing providers, monitoring, and reporting.

    and 1 more item

    Short names used here:

    • "The Registration Rules" means the National Disability Insurance Scheme (Provider Registration and Practice Standards) Rules 2018.
    • "The Incident Rules" means the National Disability Insurance Scheme (Incident Management and Reportable Incidents) Rules 2018.
    • "The Code" means the National Disability Insurance Scheme (Code of Conduct) Rules 2018, section 6.
  3. 3

    Commitments

    Numbered "We will" statements, one obligation each.

    • Holds the commitment
    C1

    We will allocate each participant a coordinator who is qualified, trained and signed off as competent for that participant's risks and complexity. A coordinator holds [qualification and years of experience we require]. The NDIA expects specialised support coordination to be delivered by a qualified and experienced practitioner, such as a social worker, psychologist, occupational therapist or mental health nurse, or a person with equivalent demonstrated experience in complex situations; set the requirement at adoption with that in mind. They have completed our training on the risk factors listed in FRM-SCO-01 Risk and Crisis Response Plan. They have supervision with the Service Manager at least every month.

    C2

    We will assess each participant's risks and complexity with them. We use their own account and the risk factors and rating scale in FRM-SCO-01 Risk and Crisis Response Plan. At the first meeting we agree interim safety steps and set a provisional risk level. We make the plan within the time for that level in PRO-SCO-02 Decision table 2, counted from the first meeting: 5 business days for extreme risk, 10 for high, 15 for medium and low. A second person, the case reviewer, reads and challenges the assessment and plan: before the plan is issued for a participant at high or extreme risk, and within 10 business days of issue for every other participant.

    C13On the thread above

    We will keep specialised support coordination separate from any other support we, or anyone connected with us, provide. We give each participant our conflict of interest policy and its plain summary in the format they use, and check they can say back what it means. We explain, and check they can say back, how coordination differs from the other supports in their plan. We take no payment, gift or benefit for naming or referring a provider. We tell the participant about any interest we hold before they choose, and their choice of provider never changes their coordination. We record every referral to or from another provider in REG-SCO-02 Support Coordination Referral Register. PRO-SCO-01 Support Coordination Conflict of Interest Procedure sets how.

    and 11 more commitments

  4. 4

    Ground rules

    Rules that bind every procedure under this policy. The only place "how" may appear.

    • Optional
    • Holds the ground rule
    G1

    The participant leads. Their words, their choices and their pace come first. We never decide for a participant who can decide for themselves.

    G2

    We share a participant's information only with the people they have agreed to. The exceptions are a call to 000, a legal duty, and a serious threat to life, health or safety when we cannot ask first. We tell the participant at the start that these exceptions exist, and tell them if we use one.

Support Coordination Conflict of Interest Procedure

  1. 1

    Purpose

    The result, and the commitment it gives effect to.

    The result: every participant who uses [Provider name] for support coordination hears our conflict of interest policy. They hear about every interest we hold in a support they may choose. They hear that their choice of provider never changes their coordination. Every referral to or from another provider is on the record.

    This procedure gives effect to POL-SCO-01 Specialised Support Coordination Policy, C13, and to the options rule in POL-SCO-02 Management of a Participant's NDIS Supports Policy, C4. It is also the support coordination part of the provider's Conflict of Interest Policy (POL-GOV-02).

    An interest is anything that [Provider name], a worker, or a person close to either could gain from a participant's choice of provider. It can be money, a business, a home, a favour or a referral arrangement. A referral is each time we pass a participant to another provider, or another provider or person passes a participant to us.

    • "coordination" means specialised support coordination
    • "the Registration Rules" means the National Disability Insurance Scheme (Provider Registration and Practice Standards) Rules 2018
    • "the Code of Conduct Rules" means the National Disability Insurance Scheme (Code of Conduct) Rules 2018.

    and 1 more item

    and 1 more paragraph

  2. 2

    When and who

    The trigger, and the role that acts.

    Start this procedure when any of these happens:

    • A person asks us for support coordination (steps 1 to 13).
    • A participant needs a support from another provider, or asks to change provider (steps 14 to 18, 20 to 22 and 24 to 27).
    • Someone refers a participant to us (steps 14, 19 to 21 and 23 to 27).

    and 1 more item

    A person who has not yet signed with us is covered in the same way as a participant (the Registration Rules, Schedule 6, clause 1(2)(a)).

    Two procedures send you here. PRO-SCO-02 Specialised Support Coordination Procedure step 14 does it before a provider is named. PRO-SCO-03 Management of a Participant's NDIS Supports Procedure step 12 does it when we hold an interest in an option. Use FRM-SCO-02 Support Options and Choice Record for the detail of each option. This procedure adds our interest, the disclosure and the referral record.

    • The coordinator does steps 1 to 11, 14 to 26 and 28.
    • The Service Manager owns this procedure and does steps 12, 13, 27 and 29.
    • The Chief Executive keeps REG-GOV-02 Conflict of Interest Register and decides each interest of [Provider name] under PRO-GOV-01 Delegations and Decision-Making Procedure.

    and 1 more item

    and 2 more paragraphs

  3. 3

    Before you start

    What must be true or to hand.

    • You can open REG-GOV-02 and REG-SCO-02 Support Coordination Referral Register.
    • You have FRM-SCO-03 Support Coordination Conflict of Interest Disclosure Record.
    • You have STA-GOV-01 Conflict of Interest: Plain Summary and STA-SCO-01 Specialised Support Coordination: Plain Summary, in the formats your participants use, and the full POL-GOV-02, where the provider has adopted them. Until then, you explain the policy and the difference in plain words (steps 4 and 5 give the words) and record "explained, no document given".

    and 3 more items

    Separation of functions: where we also provide other supports, a coordinator is not supervised by, set targets by, or paid by reference to the other service; coordination records are held apart from the other service's records; and a participant may ask for a coordinator from outside the organisation, and we help them find one. The Chief Executive records the arrangement in REG-GOV-02, and the coordinator tells the participant about it at step 6 (FRM-SCO-03 Q12).

  4. 4

    Watch out

    The few things that cause real harm if missed, each with its consequence.

    • Telling a participant late takes away their choice. A participant who hears about our interest after they choose did not choose freely.
    • A steer is a conflict even when nobody means it. Putting our service first, praising it, or leaving out other options is steering. It can breach the Code of Conduct Rules, section 6(1)(d), which requires integrity, honesty and transparency.
    • Coordination never changes because of a choice. Cutting hours, slowing replies or ending coordination after a participant picks another provider punishes them for choosing. They can complain to the NDIS Commission on 1800 035 544.

    and 3 more items

    Law changing: from 20 November 2026, a new section 73VA of the National Disability Insurance Scheme Act 2013 makes it an offence and a civil penalty for an NDIS provider to give or offer any gift or benefit likely to induce a person to use its services. Our rule already bans payment for referrals, given or taken, and is wider. Check the section when it starts.

Support Coordination Conflict of Interest Disclosure Record

  1. 1

    What this is for

    Purpose, who completes it and when, what to have ready, and what happens next.

    Use this record to show that a participant heard three things, in a way they understand. First, how we handle conflicts of interest. Second, which interests we hold in the supports they may choose. Third, that their choice of provider never changes their coordination. It also records each referral to or from another provider, and what happened to it.

    The coordinator completes it with the participant, at the time. Do not write it up later from memory.

    • Section A: every record.
    • Section B: a disclosure record. Complete it at the first meeting where supports are discussed, at each plan review, every 12 months, and when one of our interests changes.
    • Sections C and D: a referral record. Start a new copy for each referral, whether it is out or in. Complete Section C as you put the options and as the participant chooses. Complete Section D when the outcome is known and at the follow-up.

    and 1 more item

    Have ready:

    • the participant's communication needs
    • the list of our interests from REG-GOV-02 Conflict of Interest Register
    • STA-GOV-01 Conflict of Interest: Plain Summary and STA-SCO-01 Specialised Support Coordination: Plain Summary, in the participant's format, where the provider has adopted them; until then, the plain words in PRO-SCO-01 steps 4 and 5

    and 1 more item

    and 2 more paragraphs

    The collection statement, in plain words.

    Read this to the participant.

    We collect this information for three reasons. It shows that you were told about our interests before you chose. It keeps a record of each referral made for you. It meets the NDIS Practice Standards and the NDIS Code of Conduct.

    Your coordinator, the Service Manager, the Chief Executive and the Quality Manager read it. An auditor or the NDIS Commission may see it as the law allows. We share your information with another provider only when you agree, or when a law requires or allows it. FRM-INF-01 Consent to Collect, Use and Share Information Form records what you agree to.

  1. 1

    What this proves

    Purpose, the indicators it evidences, the legal basis, and the retention period with its source.

    This register is the running record of what each participant heard about our conflicts of interest, and of every referral to or from another provider. A disclosure row shows the policy we gave, the interests we told them about and the promise that their choice never changes their coordination. A referral row shows the options, our relationship to each one, what the participant chose, and what happened next.

    • a disclosure: each time we go through our conflict of interest policy and our interests with a participant

Some of these differences are meant: a policy may narrow a word for its own subject. The anatomy gives that no way to show, so a meant difference and a slip look the same.

POL-SBS-03 has no Words we use heading. Read back from the Word file, a definition sits where the heading should be.

Emphasis drifts too. Of 459 definition lines read back from the Word files, 325 bold the term, 114 bold the whole line and 20 bold nothing.

The rule proposed

  1. A term is held once, with one wording, in the Rise master.
  2. The word explains itself where it first appears in a document.
  3. The list of words in a document is made from the terms that document uses. Nobody types it.
  4. Where a document needs a narrower meaning, it says "in this policy" and still points at the shared term.

Where the rule comes from

  • 5 July 2026 Only terms actually used. Defined once, then used consistently.
  • 7 July 2026 Every hard word explains itself where it appears. No knowledge that lives only in a glossary.
  • 7 October 2026 Words we use holds references to the shared glossary.

For decision

Four rulings the anatomy is waiting on

The anatomy was drawn in July and redrawn in October. In four places the two drawings disagree, or the library does something neither drawing says.

  1. Where do definitions live?

    Before
    July: a Definitions section in every document. 7 October: references to one shared glossary.
    Now
    Each policy carries its own handwritten list, and the same term is worded differently from one policy to the next.
    Recommended
    One glossary in the Rise master. Each document's list is generated, and the word explains itself where it is first used.

  2. Where do a document's links show?

    Before
    July: a compliance links table at the top of every document, called the design centrepiece. 7 October: Authority and Related blocks near the end, generated from links.
    Now
    Authority and Related are written by hand near the end. Nothing is generated yet.
    Recommended
    Keep the reading order that puts the purpose first. Show links as chips on the passage they belong to, with Authority and Related as the printed fallback.

  3. Is a participant's plan its own kind of document?

    Before
    July: plans slot in at the level of a procedure or form. 7 October: plans need a decision, because they are the largest family in the indicators and are records about one person.
    Now
    Plan templates are written as forms. The plan kind covers only plans the organisation keeps.
    Recommended
    Decide before the linking work starts, because a plan needs its own linkable unit.

  4. Which link does a definition use?

    Before
    The taxonomy sets 25 links and none of them is for a definition.
    Now
    The library already writes "defined in" as a link.
    Recommended
    Add it to the taxonomy beside the two links proposed in July, or fold it into an existing one. One vocabulary, not two.
